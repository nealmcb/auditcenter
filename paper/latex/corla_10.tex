\subsection*{Abstract}
Colorado has an unprecedented record of transparency: a mandatory,
statewide risk-limiting audit after every election since 2017, spanning
ten years and fourteen election instances. That record has been
presented only as a fragmented set of separately published, yearly
audit-center exports -- three structurally distinct formats -- that
require substantial reconciliation and cleaning before they support
efficient analysis across elections. We reconstruct, clean, and
normalize this entire record into a single relational dataset spanning
6,522 contests, verify it against an independent ground truth by
reconstructing the state's own pseudo-random ballot selections from
public inputs alone (an exact match for 97.9\% of checkable targeted
contests), and report the resulting structural findings: 93.9\% of
contests receive at least some independently examined-ballot evidence,
and audit boards' physical reads disagreed with the voting system's own
interpretation for just 0.067\% of 965,254 examined contest-ballot
comparisons -- a rate dominated by human process error rather than any
tabulation flaw. The normalized dataset, analysis code, and a notebook
reproducing every result in this paper are available in an open-source,
independently reproducible companion repository, laying the groundwork
for the further statistical and policy work this reconstruction newly
makes possible.
